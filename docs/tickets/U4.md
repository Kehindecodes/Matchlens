## Post-break fallback (D26)

Under D15, `n` resets at every epoch boundary, so the first minutes after a goal are a second cold start. **D26 settles O9:** the thin post-break window blends against the offline `SignalPrior`, exactly as at kickoff. The pre-break match baseline is not carried over. `k` after a break stays configurable as `baseline_k_minutes_after_break`.

## Goal

Complete a `SignalReading`: `baseline`, `z_match`, `z_prior`, `trend`, `epoch_id`, from the values U3 produces.

## The maths, as settled

- `w = n / (n + k)`, with `k = settings.baseline_k_minutes` (15). At minute 4 the prior dominates; by minute 30 the match baseline does (D11).
- `baseline = w · match_mean + (1 − w) · prior_mean`
- `z_match` — deviation from this match's baseline: *something changed*
- `z_prior` — deviation from the offline prior (R1): *this match is unusual*
- Window clamped to the epoch start, `n` reset at each boundary (D15)
- After a reset, the `(1 − w)` term blends against the offline prior, as at kickoff (D26)

## Why this one is reserved

This is the time-and-state lesson in its purest form: a rolling window over an unbounded stream, with a cold start, a reset at every structural break, and two different reference frames answering two different questions. Every hard thing about event-driven systems is in this one ticket — ordering, windowing, state that must reset and must not leak across a boundary. Writing it is the point; reading someone else's is not.

## Acceptance criteria

- [x] No z-score flood in the opening minutes — assert that the first N readings do not cross the spotter's threshold on a normal match
- [x] `w` behaves: near 0 at minute 1, past 0.5 by minute 15, near 1 by minute 45
- [x] `n` resets at an epoch boundary, and the reading immediately after a goal has low `confidence` and a clamped `window_ms`
- [x] A baseline never averages across a structural break — assert directly, with a match whose second epoch has a visibly different level
- [x] `trend` agrees with the direction of the last few readings
- [x] `epoch_id` on every reading, and it is the epoch containing `clock_ms`

## Implementation choices

Made while building, none of them settled anywhere else. Change any of them freely.

- `n` counts the earlier readings in the epoch. A reading is judged first and appended after, so it is never part of its own baseline.
- The spread blends the same way as the mean: `w · match_stddev + (1 − w) · prior.stddev`, floored at 10% of the prior's stddev. `match_stddev` is the sample stddev, as R1 uses; a single reading contributes 0.
- `trend` compares the newest reading with the one two back. A change under 0.25 × spread is `steady`, and so is anything with fewer than three readings.
- `baseline_k_minutes_after_break` exists and starts at 15, the same as kickoff (D26).
- The no-flood test uses a provisional threshold, `|z_match| < 3` for the first 10 minutes, until M1 sets the spotter's.
- The `w` criterion is tested as `w(15) ≥ 0.5` and `w(45) ≥ 0.75`. With `k = 15`, `w` is exactly 0.5 at 15 and 0.75 at 45, so "past 0.5" and "near 1" hold only on that reading.
- The match tests judge against R1's committed priors (`priors.load()`).
- A placeholder (an empty window, except for chance quality) is judged but never added to the history, the same rule R1's prior follows (D28).
