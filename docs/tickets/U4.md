## ⚠️ Blocked on O9

**What does a thin post-break window blend against?** Under D15, `n` resets at every epoch boundary, so the first minutes after a goal are a second cold start. The candidates are the pre-break match baseline, the offline prior alone, or a blend of the two — and the choice changes what `z_match` *means* in the two minutes after a goal, which is exactly when the most interesting moments fire. Not startable until O9 is settled.

## Goal

Complete a `SignalReading`: `baseline`, `z_match`, `z_prior`, `trend`, `epoch_id`, from the values U3 produces.

## The maths, as settled

- `w = n / (n + k)`, with `k = settings.baseline_k_minutes` (15). At minute 4 the prior dominates; by minute 30 the match baseline does (D11).
- `baseline = w · match_mean + (1 − w) · prior_mean`
- `z_match` — deviation from this match's baseline: *something changed*
- `z_prior` — deviation from the offline prior (R1): *this match is unusual*
- Window clamped to the epoch start, `n` reset at each boundary (D15)

## Why this one is reserved

This is the time-and-state lesson in its purest form: a rolling window over an unbounded stream, with a cold start, a reset at every structural break, and two different reference frames answering two different questions. Every hard thing about event-driven systems is in this one ticket — ordering, windowing, state that must reset and must not leak across a boundary. Writing it is the point; reading someone else's is not.
