# F4 · Replay harness

**Tier** T1 · **Est** 0.5 day · **Depends on** F3 · **Phase** 0 Foundation
**Stories** J1 (a full match viewable in under five minutes)

## Goal

Load a generated match and replay it against a controllable clock at any speed, preserving order and relative timing.

## Why it matters more than it looks

No judge will watch 90 minutes in real time. Without a fast mode nobody ever sees a full match arc or reaches the recap. It is trivial now because F3's clock is a variable; it is painful to retrofit if anything assumes wall time.

## What to build

- `matchlens/source/replay.py` — takes a generated match (or a seed) and yields frames and events in `seq` order, paced against a clock.
- Speeds: 1×, 5×, 20×. A continuous multiplier is fine if simpler.
- Play, pause, and seek to a clock value.
- Seeking backwards must be safe: it re-yields from that point rather than corrupting state. If that turns out to need more than a trivial change, say so rather than building a rewind engine — it may belong in D3.
- A CLI entrypoint for your own use: `python -m matchlens.source.replay --seed X --speed 20`.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] A 90-minute match replays end to end in under 4 minutes at 20×
- [ ] Order is preserved at every speed — a test asserts `seq` is monotonic in the output at 1×, 5× and 20×
- [ ] Relative timing holds: the gap between two events scales with the speed multiplier, within tolerance
- [ ] Pause stops emission and resume continues from the same point without gaps or repeats
- [ ] Seeking to a clock emits from that clock, with nothing from before it

## Definition of done

Criteria pass, green, status updated.

## Do not

- Do not couple this to SSE or any transport. It yields; Delivery transports (D1).
- Do not add per-viewer offsets here. That is `stream_offset_ms` and it belongs to Delivery (D1) — a replay clock and a viewer clock are different things, and conflating them is how spoilers get shipped.
