# D1 · `stream_offset_ms` and the per-session release queue

**Tier** T1 · **Est** 1.5 days · **Depends on** L3, S3 · **Phase** 5 Delivery
**📝 Walkthrough required** (D29) — `docs/walkthroughs/D1.md`. This ticket carries the event-driven lesson, so its walkthrough is one of the two deepest in the set.

## Goal

A card reaches a viewer **only once their video has shown the thing it describes.** Invariant 8: *delay only, never anticipate.*

## Why it exists at all

MatchLens knows the match before the viewer does — the pipeline runs on the match clock, the viewer is some seconds behind on a stream. Release a card about a goal before the viewer's picture shows the goal and you have built a spoiler machine. Every other component can be right and this one mistake makes the product unshippable, which is why it is an invariant rather than a feature.

## What exists, and what is placeholder

Three files say *placeholder until D1* in their own docstrings:

| File | Now | Needs to become |
|---|---|---|
| `delivery/release.py` | `release_due` returns **everything** it holds | the release rule below |
| `delivery/session_clock.py` | `viewer_position` returns `match_clock_now` | `match_clock_now − session.stream_offset_ms` |
| `delivery/hub.py` | every session gets each `MatchState` as it arrives | unchanged for state; cards go through the queue |

`CardRouter.receive` already fans a card to every session on its lens and already honours `control.is_muted`. Leave it alone.

## ⚠️ The release rule is on the queue head, not on each card

```
release while  queue.head.display_at_clock <= match_clock_now − session.stream_offset_ms
```

**It is a queue, not a filter.** Scanning the whole queue and releasing every card that happens to be due will emit out of order the moment one card's `display_at_clock` is later than a card behind it — and cards can arrive out of clock order, because Rendering emits per lens and the bus does not promise ordering across moments. Release from the head, stop at the first card that is not due, and leave the rest.

This is the single most likely way to get D1 wrong, and the failure is subtle: everything arrives, slightly jumbled, and only a careful viewer notices.

## Later note · a second release gate, if enrichment goes live (D36, amended 25 Oct)

Shipped, this rule has one gate. Once A2 runs a **real** model against a live match and viewers hold **differing** offsets, it needs a second: release at `max(viewer's due time, the moment's enrichment deadline)`, so a moment's extra claims either reach every viewer's card or none of them. Without it, a later viewer's card is richer than an earlier viewer's for the same moment, which is the thing D2 asserts must not happen.

Legal under invariant 8 because delaying further is always permitted; only early release is forbidden. Not outstanding work while A2 runs scripted.

## What to build

- The real release rule above, per session (D7: Delivery is a **per-session scheduler, not a push** — each session has its own queue and its own clock position).
- `viewer_position` returning the viewer's actual position in the match.
- `last_seen_clock` updated as cards release, so a rejoin does not replay what was already seen — the field's own docstring says that is its job.
- **Reconnect resumes rather than replays.** SSE gives `Last-Event-ID` for free, and D5 chose SSE partly because that id maps onto this buffer. A reconnecting viewer picks up after their last card, not from kickoff.
- Keep `stream_offset_ms` in **match-clock milliseconds**, never wall clock. At 25× replay a 45-second offset passes in under two seconds of real time, and the rule still has to be right.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] **No card is ever released early.** Construct a session at a 45s offset and assert no card arrives before `match_clock_now − 45_000` reaches its `display_at_clock`. This is V8 and the ticket's reason for existing
- [ ] **Cards release in `display_at_clock` order**, including when they arrive out of order. Enqueue three cards out of order and assert the release order — this is the head-of-queue test
- [ ] One card not yet due blocks the cards behind it, rather than being skipped
- [ ] `last_seen_clock` advances on release, and a rejoin replays nothing already seen
- [ ] A reconnect with `Last-Event-ID` resumes after that card and does not start over
- [ ] Two sessions on the same lens with different offsets hold different queue depths at the same instant
- [ ] Nothing in the queue is dropped: a card held at the final whistle still releases once its viewer's clock reaches it
- [ ] Deterministic under replay: same seed and offsets, same release sequence

## Definition of done

Criteria pass, `ruff` and `pytest` green, status updated, **and `docs/walkthroughs/D1.md` written** — covering where the per-session state lives, why the rule is on the head, and what breaks if it is not.

## Do not

- Do not anticipate, ever, for any reason — not to smooth a gap, not to catch a viewer up. Catch-up is D4 and it summarises; it does not release early.
- Do not use wall clock anywhere in the rule.
- Do not reorder, merge or drop cards to make a stream look smoother. Renditions are a record of what was shown.
- Do not push from the producer side. Each session pulls on its own clock (D7).
