## 1 · Open with the five stages, because the brief asks for exactly that

The challenge says *"one pipeline with five stages"* and Parallax is those five stages in order. Lead the written pitch with the mapping — it is the strongest structural move available:

| The brief asks | Parallax |
|---|---|
| (a) ingest synthetic events as they happen | Match Source → Ingest |
| (b) interpret into stats, patterns and context | Understanding — possessions, shot quality, four signals, baselines, epochs |
| (c) explain **why** a moment matters | Spotting → Explanation (L7 rules + A2) |
| (d) render it to sit on screen alongside the match | Rendering → Delivery, `OverlayPacket` |
| (e) personalize so an analyst and a casual fan get genuinely different experiences | Selection + the lens model |

Then quote the brief's own warning back at it: *"a Project that only generates metrics has no audience, and a solution that only personalizes has nothing to personalize."* Parallax does both, and that sentence is the argument for its shape.

Judges are not required to watch past two minutes, so the thesis goes early and the cut is timed, not estimated. Target **110 seconds** on the edit so the upload lands under the limit with room.

**Load-bearing: 0:08, 0:44, 1:00.** The thesis, the adjudication, the resilience. If the cut runs long — and the first attempt will — trim in this order: 1:24, 1:36, 0:28. Never trim the three above to save the three below.

**No music.** Voiceover with burned-in captions. That removes the copyright exposure in section 5 completely, survives a judge watching muted, and carries more in 110 seconds than music ever would.

## 4 · Say these in the written materials. All of them.

Each is a thing a judge would otherwise find, and finding beats being told. These are **written-submission** material — only two of them fit the video, and the cut above places them: the deterministic adjudicator at 0:44 and the ladder at 1:00.

- **Synthetic data is compliance, not apology.** The brief requires it. The seeded generator, four archetypes and determinism are deliverables against that requirement.
- **The 25× replay serves pre-computed A2 claims (D36).** The live path calls the model inside the viewer's offset; 25× compresses that budget twenty-five-fold, so the replay serves a cache. Stated, obviously correct; unstated, it is the thing that gets found.
- **Two LLM agents, not three (D35).** A2 the explainer and A4 the renderer. **A3 is deterministic** — six recomputations and set comparisons — because a model-based verifier would itself be unverifiable. The headline is not an agent count: *a model-assisted pipeline in which a deterministic adjudicator can overrule the model and records why.*
- **No database (D38).** v1 needs none because the source is a seed; production needs one because the source is a feed. Event sourcing doing its job.
- **Streaming, not broadcast (D34).** Overlays composited onto a feed are one-to-many, which collapses personalization. The packet is the in-player form, rendered per session.
- **`z_prior` means unusual *for this generator*** (R1), not for football. The mechanism is right and the reference population is synthetic — two separate claims.
- **The package is still `matchlens`** (D37). One line; the name changed, the imports did not.

## 5 · Third-party trademarks and music: the synthetic data is the defence

The rules bar third-party trademarks and copyrighted music without permission. **The generator already closed the largest gap**: `source/squads.py` assembles team names from invented places and clubs (`Northgate Rovers`) and player names from syllables rather than a surname list, and says so in its docstring. The synthetic-data requirement and this rule point the same way, and nothing in the data layer needs changing.

What is left is everything around the data:

- **No match footage.** Nothing composited over real broadcast video, and no clip of a real match behind the overlay demo. The `OverlayPacket` shot proves the form against a plain or synthetic backdrop.
- **No music.** See section 3. Voiceover and captions only.
- **Check the page chrome, not the fixtures.** The frontend, the page title, the favicon and any header image are the one place a real crest or league mark could still have been pasted in for flavour. Open the deployed page and look before recording.

Naming the competition and the hackathon in the pitch is fine — that is what the entry is for. Dressing the product in a league's marks is not.

## 6 · Making the repository public

The repo must be public or the entry is not considered, and flipping it is the moment every secret in the history becomes readable. Before, not after:

- **Sweep for committed secrets** — `.env` first, then `git log -p` for `foundry_endpoint`, `appinsights_connection_string`, `service_bus_connection`, `database_connection`, the deployed hostname and the registry name. `settings.py` is the only reader of the environment, which is what makes this sweep tractable.
- **History counts, not just the working tree.** A key removed in a later commit is still in the clone. If one is in there, rotate it rather than rewriting history the day before submission.
- **Then** make it public, and open the public URL from a signed-out browser to confirm.
- README at the top: what it is, the deployed URL, how to run it on the demo seed, and the measured numbers.
