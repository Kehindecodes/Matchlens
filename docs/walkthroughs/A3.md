# A3 walkthrough

**Every claim, L7's and the model's alike, gets all six checks against the record as it stood when the claim was checked, and a verdict that follows from them. A check that raises, a claim that does not validate, and a claim that runs out of budget all fail closed. Nothing about a claim is ever changed.**

## What had to be decided

- **What a claim is checked against.** Not the explainer's frozen log. If the verifier read only the frozen log, a claim citing a later event could not *resolve* it, so `citation` would fail it and `temporal` would never fire. So `verify` reads a `Record`: the whole log and the readings at the time of the check, as a snapshot. The snapshot is also what makes it safe on the model's thread (Q34: A).
- **The signature grew.** It was `verify(claim, events)` and is now `verify(claim, moment, claims, record)`. `temporal` needs the moment's clock. `coverage` (D22) is a property of the *moment*, so it needs all of the moment's claims. Every claim of a moment gets the same coverage answer, so when coverage fails, **every claim of that moment fails**, and the moment's cards are suppressed as `verification_failed`. That is fail-closed applied to D22. It never fires on L7.
- **`value` against `entailment`** (D21) is held by what each one reads. `value` recomputes from `record.events` and `record.readings` and never looks at `evidence_ids`. `entailment` looks only at the rows the claim cites. They share the D30 definitions and nothing else. Each has a test of its own, and one test proves they differ: the right number with unrelated citations passes `value` and fails `entailment`.
- **Two answers from you.** **Q35, tolerance:** counts exact, everything else within `1e-6` relative. **Q36, entity:** "on the pitch across the window", taken literally. That needed L7 to change: a substitute's goals window now starts when he came on, and the rules version is now `claims-2`.
- **The budget against determinism.** A claim over `verifier_budget_ms` fails its remaining checks, as the brief asks. But a timeout depends on the wall clock, and a failed claim leaves its card, so a tight budget would make seeded cards vary with machine load (invariant 9). The slowest claim across 12 matches took 23 ms. The budget is 500 ms, so it catches a check that has gone pathological, not a slow afternoon.
- **A figure that cannot be recomputed is `NaN`** in `recomputed_value`, with the reason given. `recomputed_value` has one entry per operand, and leaving the tuple short would hide which operand failed.

## What broke on the way

- **Nothing in L7.** All 1,411 L7 claims across 12 generated matches passed all six checks on the first run, and every seed-1 card in all four archetypes is byte-identical to before A3. That is not luck: L7's tests already recount every value from its cited rows (`test_builder._recount`), which is `entailment` written as a test.
- **The real bug was in the brief, and was caught before any code ran:** "across the window" against L7's `[0, clock]` scorer window. **6 of 27 goals** are scored by substitutes. If I had implemented the brief as written, `entity` would have stripped "his first goal" from every one of them (Q36).
- **The tracing test flaked again.** The checks allocate, so young-generation garbage collections ran inside the timed run. The fixture now pauses the collector as well as freezing the heap.
- **A test that passed for the wrong reason.** I gutted `entailment`'s operand rule, and "a signal operand citing an event" still failed entailment. It had picked an interpretive claim, which fails a different branch. It now uses a comparative claim, and the mutant fails it.

## What a reader of this code would get wrong

- **Each check judges the claim as a whole,** not one operand. A comparative claim with one bad operand fails, and the reason names that operand.
- **`recompute` returns a list** because two shots can share a millisecond. The closest candidate is compared, and the recorded figure is that candidate.
- **`build_up_passes` is recomputed from events up to the window's end, not the whole log.** A move can keep going after the shot (a rebound kept), so counting over the whole log would add passes the claim never meant.
- **Which check catches the model's mistakes:** in the live run (`docs/evidence/A2-live-seed1.txt`), the wrong counts are `value` failures. The `possession_control` operand citing an event, and `goals` 0 citing non-goals, are `entailment` failures. I worked that out from the evidence. The live run has not been put through A3 again.

## The test that matters

`test_a_right_number_with_unrelated_citations_fails_entailment_and_passes_value`. If it fails, the six checks have become five. Close behind it is `test_no_card_depends_on_a2_or_waits_for_it[Liar]`: A3 rejects every number the liar changed, while L7's claims on the same moments pass and their cards ship. That is D35's demo shot, in Audit.

## If you change this later

Bump `MODEL_VERSION` (`checks-1`) whenever a check changes. Never let `value` read `evidence_ids`. If the budget ever starts failing claims, look at the check before you raise the number: a claim that fails on time drops off its card on one machine and not on another.
